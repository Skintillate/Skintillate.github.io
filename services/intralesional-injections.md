## Conditions treated with intralesional injections

- **Keloids and hypertrophic scars**: raised, thick scars after ear piercing, acne on the chest or shoulders, surgery, burns or vaccination.
- **Alopecia areata**: round bald patches on the scalp, beard or eyebrows.
- **Painful acne cysts and nodules**, to help them settle quickly and reduce scarring.
- **Thick, stubborn patches** of psoriasis, lichen planus, prurigo and similar conditions.
- **Nail psoriasis** and other inflammatory nail conditions.

## Medicines used

The most common medicine is a corticosteroid such as triamcinolone, which reduces inflammation and flattens thick scar tissue. For some keloids, it may be combined with other medicines, such as 5-fluorouracil, for a better response. The choice and strength depend on the condition, its location and your skin.

## What to expect

1. **Assessment.** Dr. Kiruthika examines the area and explains how many sessions are likely to be needed.
2. **Injection.** The skin is cleaned and the medicine is injected with a very fine needle. It takes only a few minutes.
3. **Repeat sessions.** Injections are usually repeated every 3 to 6 weeks, depending on the condition, until the desired improvement is reached.

### Possible side effects
Mild pain at the injection site, temporary thinning, lightening or a slight dip in the skin. These are minimised by careful dosing and usually improve over time.

## Keloids need a combined approach

Keloids tend to come back, especially if they are simply cut out. Injections work best combined with silicone gel or sheets and, for some areas such as earlobes, pressure devices. Avoid new piercings or tattoos if you are prone to keloids.
