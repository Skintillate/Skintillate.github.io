## What chemical peels can help with

- Active acne, blackheads and whiteheads
- Acne marks and mild scars
- Melasma, tanning and uneven skin tone
- Dull, tired-looking skin
- Oily skin and open pores
- Fine lines and early signs of sun damage
- Dark underarms, neck and elbows

## Types of peels we use

| Peel | Best suited for |
|---|---|
| Salicylic acid | Oily, acne-prone skin, blackheads and whiteheads |
| Glycolic acid | Dullness, fine lines, uneven tone and pigmentation |
| Lactic and mandelic acid | Sensitive skin and pigmentation |
| Combination peels (such as Jessner's) | Stubborn pigmentation and melasma |
| TCA (low strength, and focal CROSS technique) | Marks, fine lines and ice-pick acne scars |

The peel, its strength and how long it is left on are chosen for your skin type and concern, and adjusted as your skin responds.

## What to expect

1. **Preparation.** For some peels you will use a prescribed cream for 2 to 4 weeks beforehand to prepare your skin.
2. **The peel.** Your face is cleansed, the peel is applied for a few minutes and then neutralised or removed. You may feel tingling or mild warmth.
3. **Afterwards.** Mild redness for a few hours and light flaking for a few days are normal.

### Aftercare
- Use sunscreen every day and avoid direct sun.
- Use a gentle cleanser and moisturiser.
- Avoid scrubs, waxing and retinoid creams for a few days.
- Do not pick or peel flaking skin.

## Who should avoid peels

Peels are postponed if you have an active cold sore, infection, open wounds or sunburn on the face. Tell us if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, have recently taken isotretinoin, or have very sensitive skin or eczema; Dr. Kiruthika will advise whether a peel suits you.
